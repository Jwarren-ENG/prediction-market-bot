# Which Bitcoin trading bots win?

Research checked November 26, 2026. For a $10,000 account trading five-minute, fifteen-minute or hourly Bitcoin event contracts, I found no independently verified bot leaderboard that compares the same windows, executable prices, capital and costs on both US venues. Published studies do answer narrower questions. The strongest direct comparison I found of named AI traders gives an instructive result. Its most accurate live Polymarket entrant lost money.

## A named bot with a published win rate

Prediction Arena tested agents on multiple event categories. The authors gave them $10,000 per account and let them make decisions every 15–45 minutes. Their live Polymarket cohort ran from February 9 through March 9, 2026. The table below reproduces selected reported results. The win rate counts settled positions. The trade count includes entries and exits, so dividing wins by that column would give the wrong rate. These agents could choose among Polymarket markets. The paper supplies no performance breakdown for Polymarket US Bitcoin at the intervals in this app. [Prediction Arena, methods and Table 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.07355v1#S7.SS4)

| Agent in that study | Capital and interval | Reported settlement win rate | Trades | Account return |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Grok 4.20 checkpoint | Live, Feb 9–Mar 9 | 71.4% | 18 | −1.85% |
| GPT-5.2 | Live, Feb 9–Mar 9 | 43.5% | 127 | −0.42% |
| GLM-4.7 | Live, Feb 9–Mar 9 | 10.5% | 95 | −0.09% |
| Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview | Paper, Mar 6–9 | 50.0% | 76 | +6.02% |

The six original Kalshi agents lost between 16.0% and 30.8% over the full 57-day evaluation. The selection opportunities and observation periods differed across venues. Gemini's three days of paper results provide a short experiment. The live cohort provides the better test of execution. Neither establishes a model to buy or hand an account to. [Prediction Arena, sections 6–7](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.07355v1#S6)

## Which trading approaches have evidence?

| Approach | Published evidence | Implication for this desk |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Buying heavily favored outcomes | Bürgi, Deng and Whelan studied over 300,000 Kalshi contracts. Cheap contracts tended to underperform their implied probabilities. Higher-priced contracts performed better in their broad historical sample. | Track the price paid for the favorite. Nine winning $1 settlements from ten 95¢ purchases return $9 on $9.50 of purchases, a 50¢ loss before fees. A broad favorite effect needs fresh testing on each Bitcoin interval. [CESifo paper](https://www.ifo.de/DocDL/cesifo1_wp12122.pdf) |
| Posting bids and offers | The same Kalshi study found distinct historical outcomes for traders supplying quotes and traders accepting them. A posted quote waits in a queue and exposes the trader to more informed incoming orders. | A paper maker needs queue position, actual trade-through volume, cancellations and adverse price movement. Merely touching a displayed bid cannot establish a fill. The current trials take available liquidity. [CESifo paper](https://www.ifo.de/DocDL/cesifo1_wp12122.pdf) |
| Combining contracts with complementary payouts | Saguillo and colleagues estimated about $39.6 million of extracted arbitrage across on-chain Polymarket markets resolving between April 2024 and April 2025. Their analysis identifies executed opportunities and concentration among accounts. | This is historical aggregate profit. Capital committed, failed attempts, transfer costs and the success rate of a new retail bot are different quantities. Different opening times mean the five-minute and fifteen-minute Bitcoin contracts can both legitimately resolve in opposite directions. [Authors’ study, sections 4 and 7](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.03474) |
| Predicting five-minute direction from price | Gruener examined 14,808 contracts over 16 days on international Polymarket across four crypto assets. Posted probabilities roughly matched realized frequencies. The author identifies extra information in public prices and explicitly describes the logged quote as non-executable. | Any lead from a spot price must survive executable asks, bid depth, fees, sampling delay and each venue’s settlement methodology. This study cannot establish profitable fills on the US products. [Revised five-minute study](https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.6863546) |
| Asking multiple AI agents to trade | Prediction Arena records actual losses alongside high settlement accuracy. In a separate Kalshi study of more than a billion trades, 0.15% of takers met the authors’ strict test of superior forecasting ability. The authors distinguish that forecasting measure from profit. | AI can research rules and propose testable hypotheses. A public win-rate claim or an agreement among models supplies no execution record. [Prediction Arena](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.07355) · [Kalshi Research](https://kalshi.com/research/publications/who-calibrates-markets) |

## Reading the three $10,000 results

Each contract account starts with $10,000 in paper cash. The five-minute and hourly accounts use Polymarket US. The fifteen-minute account uses Kalshi. The same predeclared order-book momentum rule runs on each when enabled. It can stay in cash for an entire window. The worker records the market ID, timing, executable levels, fees and the venue's official outcome for any position held through expiry. The data sources and fee assumptions differ across venues. [Kalshi crypto contract rules](https://help.kalshi.com/en/articles/13823838-crypto-markets) · [Polymarket US Bitcoin markets](https://polymarket.us/category/btc) · [Polymarket US fee schedule](https://docs.polymarket.us/fees)

At the Eastern day boundary, compare fee-adjusted equity with each account’s starting mark. Read closed-window gains alongside fees, money at risk, open positions and any unavailable liquidation marks. Report the number of independent traded windows and the number closed. Several fills within one window count as one outcome. One hour offers twelve five-minute windows, four fifteen-minute windows and one hourly window. They share the same underlying Bitcoin moves. A one-evening ranking is descriptive, and a ten-trade winning streak still gives very little information about the next hundred trades.

For a follow-up experiment, freeze one challenger rule before collecting new data. Use the same books and fee assumptions as the current rule, include all eligible windows and keep the earlier run intact. Compare against holding cash. For uncertainty, group outcomes by day so that overlapping Bitcoin moves stay together when resampling. A claim about earning money needs a positive after-cost return on later dates, manageable losses and enough independent observations for the size of the proposed edge. [AlphaOpsBench, execution and fee sensitivity](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31390)
